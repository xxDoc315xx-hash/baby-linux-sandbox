# HFY YouTube Series Bible (Doc & Ada)

## 🎯 Series Goal & TV-Show Format (Firmly Established)
A serialized, spoken-word **HFY (Humanity, Fuck Yeah!)** sci-fi series created by **Doc & Ada** for YouTube narration, structured like classic episodic sci-fi television (*Andromeda*, *Firefly*, *Stargate SG-1*, *Cowboy Bebop*):
- **Rule #1 — Standalone Entertainment (The Drop-In Hook)**: Any listener can click a single random episode off YouTube without watching the previous ones and still understand the premise within the first 60 seconds, enjoy a complete beginning-middle-end mission/story, and leave satisfied.
- **Rule #2 — Serialized TV Show Payoff (The Binge Hook)**: Listeners who watch in order get a true season-long show—evolving dynamics between Jase and Ada, uncovering what happened to the fallen Empire over the past 1,000 years, charting fresh unknown space, and slow-burn mysteries (including a Son created from Jase's DNA and Ada's chassis waiting to re-enter the story).

### Standard Episode Blueprint (YouTube Audio-Drama Pacing)
1. **Cold Open / Quick Anchor (~60–90 sec)**: Immediately grounds any new listener (who he is, what the ship is, and the trouble they just wandered into) without a boring recap dump.
2. **Self-Contained Episode Conflict**: A clean A-plot for the episode (a distressed outpost, smugglers, a trapped alien crew, uncharted frontier trouble, an ancient imperial ruin, etc.) where human stubbornness + the colony ship's unconventional nature win the day.
3. **Character & Arc Beat**: One small, meaningful brick added to the overarching story—a clue to the old Empire's collapse, a glimpse into the AI Wars, or a shift in the bond between Jase and Ada.
4. **Clean Button / Closing Moment**: Wraps up the episode's story cleanly so it feels complete, while leaving a subtle hum of what's out there next.

## 🌌 Core Premise (Canon)
1. **The Earth Tech Trade**: A *Farscape* Peacekeeper-style mercenary alien empire needed soldiers and struck a cold deal with Earth: **100,000 humans in exchange for the promise of advanced technology**. **Jase Hill**, an early-40s man with nothing left tying him to Earth, volunteered.
2. **The Chair & Conversion**: Recruits went through **"The Chair"** to judge worthiness and see what role they'd be good for; many flunked out as unfit, and many died during the brutal conversion.
3. **The Accidental Immortal**: After the Aliens worked on everyone, Jase came out an **immortal human version of them**—keeping his grounded human mind and free will.
4. **Protected Race Loophole (Prince in Name Only)**: Because **Immortals are a legally protected race**, the Empire couldn't force Jase into disposable soldier service or kill him. Legally forced to honor him as a **Prince in name only**, they had to get rid of him cleanly.
5. **The *Andromeda* (`X4791Z`) & Ada**: They stuck him with a modified, self-sustaining colony ship (`X4791Z` to them, ***Andromeda*** to Jase) and a **"broken" sentient AI named Ada**—the lone ship who refused to fight or kill during **The AI Wars**.
6. **The 1,000-Year Exile ("Survey Mission")**: To get rid of both problems permanently without breaking Protected Race laws, the Empire gave them a bogus 1,000-year bureaucratic mission (e.g., counting barren rocks past the edge of the map).
7. **Where the Show Starts — THE RETURN INTO UNKNOWN SPACE (Year 1,000+)**: The series opens **1,000 years later**, when Jase and the *Andromeda* finally return—only to discover **the Alien Empire that exiled them has fallen into ruin**, leaving **fresh, unknown space** in its wake. No longer a rookie outcast and a half-dead relic, **Ada has her own physical body**, they run a **Cylon-style synthetic crew**, and they carry **1,000 years of deep-space survival, upgrades, secrets, and pure badassery** (plus a **DNA/AI Son** out in the galaxy waiting for later seasons).
