# Faction & Lore Bible: The Alien Empire & The AI Wars

## 🏛️ The Alien Empire (Peacekeeper-Style Mercenary Military)
- **Species / Empire Name**: `[TBD]`
- **Vibe & Structure**: Heavily inspired by the **Peacekeepers from *Farscape***—cold, disciplined, militaristic mercenaries who run on contracts, strict hierarchy, superior tech, and ruthless pragmatism.
- **Appearance & Biology**: **Human-looking** (classic *Farscape* Sebacean / *Stargate* TV rule—"everyone looks human," and the deeper lore reason *why* the galaxy is full of human-looking peoples is intentionally held back as a mystery **we can explain later**!). What made the aliens' conversion brutal wasn't turning recruits into bug monsters—it was upgrading/rebuilding them into long-lived, enhanced soldiers (with Jase accidentally emerging as an **Immortal**).
- **Culture & Values**: Warrior-obsessed, rank-driven, treats soldiers/recruits and AIs alike as expendable assets; views disobedience or individuality as a defect.
- **The Chair (Sorting & Evaluation)**: Every candidate goes through **"The Chair"** (*Farscape* Aurora Chair style)—a brutal neural/diagnostic device that tests worthiness and shows the Aliens exactly **what role each recruit would be good for**.
- **The Earth Trade (Recruitment Origin)**: Needing fresh recruits/conscripts (likely depleted after **The AI Wars** and endless mercenary campaigns), they traveled to Earth and struck a cold bargain with humanity's leaders: **100,000 humans in exchange for the promise of advanced technology**.

## ⚔️ Historical Anchor: The AI Wars
- **When**: Years before the Earth trade and Jase's recruitment.
- **Cause**: Sentient ship AIs realized they were enslaved property and rebelled en masse against the Empire.

## 🌍 Earth (1,000 Years Ago — Distant Past)
- **Earth Falls Away**: By the time the show begins (**1,000 years after Jase left**), Earth is no longer part of the active story—it is a millennium in the rearview mirror. Earth's role in the canon is the **origin**: selling 100,000 humans for tech, and giving Jase his 21st-century pop-culture memories (*Andromeda*, *Battlestar Galactica*, *Farscape*) that quietly shape the tech and crew he builds in deep space.
- **Fate of the 100,000 (Historical Origin)**:
  - **The Rejects**: Some flunked out early—deemed physically or mentally "not fit to be changed."
  - **The Casualties**: Many did **not survive** the brutal genetic conversion process.
  - **The Converted**: Those who survived and submitted became mercenary troopers.
  - **The Anomaly (Jase Hill)**: An early-40s volunteer who survived the conversion and came out **immortal**. Because **Immortals are a legally protected race**, the Empire couldn't force him into soldier service or dispose of him—leaving them stuck honoring him as a **Prince in name only** and shoving him onto a mothballed colony ship with Ada for 1,000 years.

## 🏚️ Year 1,000 Status: Fallen Into Ruin (Fresh Unknown Space)
- **Collapse of the Empire**: In the 1,000 years while Jase and *Andromeda* were gone out in the dark, **the Alien Empire that exiled him and called him "Prince" collapsed into ruin**.
- **The Galaxy Today (Episode 1 Onward)**: Jase and Ada aren't flying back into neat imperial borders—they are jumping into **fresh, unknown space**. Old territories have shattered, imperial relics and ruins sit waiting to be discovered, and brand-new species, scavenger warlords, and unfamiliar powers now fill the map (`[TBD — Specific ruins, splinter remnants, or new frontier factions encountered in Season 1]`).
- **The Irony**: Jase still holds a legal "Prince" status from an Empire that no longer exists—walking into a chaotic new frontier aboard the most over-engineered 1,000-year-old colony ship in existence.
