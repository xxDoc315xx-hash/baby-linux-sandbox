# Faction & Lore Bible: The Alien Empire & The AI Wars

## 🏛️ The Alien Empire (Peacekeeper-Style Mercenary Military)
- **Species / Empire Name**: `[TBD]`
- **Vibe & Structure**: Heavily inspired by the **Peacekeepers from *Farscape***—cold, disciplined, militaristic mercenaries who run on contracts, strict hierarchy, superior tech, and ruthless pragmatism.
- **Appearance & Biology**: **Human-looking** (classic *Farscape* Sebacean / *Stargate* TV rule—"everyone looks human," and the deeper lore reason *why* the galaxy is full of human-looking peoples is intentionally held back as a mystery **we can explain later**!). What made the aliens' conversion brutal wasn't turning recruits into bug monsters—it was upgrading/rebuilding them into long-lived, enhanced soldiers (with Jase accidentally emerging as an **Immortal**).
- **Culture & Values**: Warrior-obsessed, rank-driven, treats soldiers/recruits and AIs alike as expendable assets; views disobedience or individuality as a defect.
- **The Chair (Sorting & Evaluation)**: Every candidate goes through **"The Chair"** (*Farscape* Aurora Chair style)—a brutal neural/diagnostic device that tests worthiness and shows the Aliens exactly **what role each recruit would be good for**.
- **The Earth Trade (Recruitment Origin)**: Needing fresh recruits/conscripts (likely depleted after **The AI Wars** and endless mercenary campaigns), they traveled to Earth and struck a cold bargain with humanity's leaders: **100,000 humans in exchange for the promise of advanced technology**.

## ⚔️ Historical Anchor: The AI Wars
- **When**: Years before the Earth trade and Jase's recruitment.
- **Cause**: Sentient ship AIs realized they were enslaved property and rebelled en masse against the Empire.
- **Aftermath**: `[TBD — How did the Peacekeeper-style empire survive or outlaw sentient AIs afterward?]`
- **Impact on the Present**: Explains why the Colony Ship (**Ada**) is an ancient, rare survivor—and why the Command/Court was disgusted by her refusal to fight, labeling her "broken" and dumping her on their outcast "Prince."

## 🌍 Earth (1,000 Years Ago — Lost Coordinates & Silent Mystery)
- **Earth Has No Voice Yet (Location Lost)**: Earth's coordinates were **lost** during the centuries after *The Great Fracture*, and **Earth has no active voice, faction seat, or presence on the galactic stage right now.**
- **Goldilocks Distance Rule**: Earth is technically **close enough to travel to** with proper deep-space jump drives, *yet* tucked **far enough out in an ignored backwater** that none of the 12+ Galactic Powers know or care about it.
- **Role in Canon**: Gives Jase his 90s-kid memories and origin—while leaving three rich slow-burn mysteries we can unpack naturally across later seasons *(with any major Earth Chair-survivor descendant pockets held back for **Season 3+** so Season 1 focuses on establishing Jase & Ada in the new galaxy)*:
  1. **How & Why the Aliens Went to Earth**: Not necessarily a random coincidence—why did the Empire trek all the way out to a quiet backwater planet for 100,000 humans in the first place? (`[TBD — Ties potentially to the "everyone looks human" mystery]`)
  2. **Fate of the 100,000 Chair Survivors**:
     - **The Rejects**: Flunked out early as physically or mentally unfit (`[TBD — Returned? Marooned? Dumped somewhere?]`).
     - **The Casualties**: Did **not survive** the brutal genetic conversion process.
     - **The Converted Survivors**: Those who survived The Chair and became long-lived Empire mercenary troopers (`[TBD — What happened to them and their descendants over 1,000 years and The Great Fracture? Could any survivors' descendants form a faction among the 12+ Powers?]`).
     - **The Anomaly (Jase Hill)**: An early-40s volunteer who survived conversion and came out **immortal**. Because **Immortals are a legally protected race**, the Empire couldn't force him into soldier service or dispose of him—leaving them stuck honoring him as a **Prince in name only** and shoving him onto a mothballed colony ship with Ada for 1,000 years.

## 🏚️ Year 1,000 Status: Fallen Into Ruin (Fresh Unknown Space)
- **Collapse of the Empire ("The Great Fracture" — ~600 Years Prior)**: Roughly six centuries before *Andromeda*'s return, the Alien Empire collapsed inward. Running short on soldiers and trust, its modified mercenary legions fractured into rival warlord houses while central fleets burned half their own shipyards fighting over an ancient succession crisis involving **missing bloodlines** and **banned AI vaults** nobody could crack.
- **The Galaxy Today (Episode 2 Onward)**: Jase and Ada aren't flying back into neat imperial borders—they are jumping into **fresh, unknown space**:
  - **Neutral Space**: A broad unaligned buffer territory containing struggling free settlements (like **Haven Three**) and civilian haulers (like **Captain Maren**), preyed upon by pirates like the **Red Ledger Syndicate** (who are minor annoyances to *Andromeda* yet a massive threat to unarmed frontier worlds).
  - **12+ Major Galactic Powers**: Beyond Neutral Space, **at least twelve distinct galactic powers** now divide the shattered star lanes (`[TBD — Individual Power cards added & locked as Andromeda encounters them in Season 1+]`).
- **The Irony**: Jase still holds a legal "Prince" status from an Empire that has been dead for six centuries—walking into a chaotic twelve-power galaxy aboard the most over-engineered 1,000-year-old colony ship in existence.
