# Character Bible: Jase Hill (The Immortal Prince)

## 🪪 Core Identity
- **Name**: **Jase Hill**
- **Age**: Left Earth in his early 40s; **1,000+ years old** by the time Episode 1 begins (looks prime/ageless, carries a millennium of calm experience)
- **Title**: Prince (In name only — legally protected status because **Immortals are a protected race**)
- **Species**: Uplifted / Hybridized Human (Immortal human version of the alien species)
- **Role**: Returning "Failed Warrior," Captain of the *Andromeda* (`X4791Z`)

## 🧬 Origin & "Failure"
- **How He Was Recruited**: Earth traded **100,000 humans** to the Peacekeeper-style mercenary empire in exchange for **advanced alien technology**. Jase, in his early 40s with nothing left tying him to Earth, **volunteered** for the transfer.
- **The Chair (Evaluation & Sorting)**: Every recruit is strapped into **"The Chair"** (inspired by *Farscape*'s Aurora Chair)—a neural/aptitude machine that probes their mind and biology to judge if they are worthy and reveal **what role they would be good for**. Some flunked out immediately as unfit; others didn't survive the conversion.
- **The Transformation**: After the Aliens worked on everyone, Jase didn't turn into an obedient grunt—the conversion made him an **immortal human version of them**.
- **The Legal Loophole & 1,000-Year Exile**: In the galaxy, **Immortals are a legally protected race**. The Aliens couldn't force Jase into cannon-fodder service or execute him. Stuck honoring his protected status as a **Prince in name only**, they handed him a mothballed colony ship (`X4791Z` / Ada) and sent both of them on a bogus **1,000-year survey mission** to get rid of them.
- **The Return**: **1,000 years later**, Jase and Ada come back into known space—hardened, upgraded, accompanied by a Cylon-style crew, and carrying a millennium of deep-space experience.

## 🧠 Personality & Voice
- **Core Traits**: Grounded, unshakeable, dry-humored; 1,000 years in space didn't make him a pompous space-god—it made him extraordinarily calm, dangerous when pushed, and fiercely protective of Ada and his ship.
- **Pop-Culture / Aesthetic Influence**: Being an ordinary Earth guy from the modern era, Jase's whole blueprint for "cool sci-fi" came from the TV shows he used to watch (*Andromeda*, *Battlestar Galactica*, *Farscape*). When he and Ada started building drones, synthetic bodies, and ship upgrades out in the dark, he modeled them straight off classic Earth sci-fi—producing **Cylon-style** synthetic crew and *Andromeda*-inspired systems that utterly baffle aliens!

## ⚔️ Abilities & Limitations
- **Strengths**: Immortality, alien-tier resilience, **1,000 years of combat, survival, and engineering experience** in uncharted deep space, protected legal status, and total synergy with Ada and their Cylon crew.

## 🤝 Key Relationships
- **With Ada (The Ship AI)**: Sees her not as broken hardware or a slave, but as his true partner and equal—someone else the Empire tried to lock away on a technicality. Early in Year 1, Jase knows **nothing** about AIs or the AI Wars (and Ada doesn't bring up her warship past), which makes the early *"Ship vs. Jase"* hatch standoffs hilariously grounded (Jase: *"I'm going out to get..."* / Ada: *locks hatch* **"No, you aren't. If you leave and get stuck, I'd be all alone."**). Strictly **platonic, ride-or-die partnership** (Jase's grounded Rule #1: never get romantically messy with the person who controls life support, gravity, and every airlock—and Ada's counter-rule: *nobody walks off alone and leaves the other stranded with mute maintenance bots!*).
- **With the Alien Court**: Mutual annoyance. They accidentally minted a legally untouchable human "Prince" they hate; he has zero interest in their military games.
