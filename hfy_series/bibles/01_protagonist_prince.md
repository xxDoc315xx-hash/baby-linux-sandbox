# Character Bible: Jase Hill (The Immortal Prince)

## 🪪 Core Identity
- **Name**: **Jase Hill**
- **Age**: Left Earth in his early 40s; **1,000+ years old** by the time Episode 1 begins (looks prime/ageless, carries a millennium of calm experience)
- **Title**: Prince (In name only — legally protected status because **Immortals are a protected race**)
- **Species**: Uplifted / Hybridized Human (Immortal human version of the alien species)
- **Role**: Returning "Failed Warrior," Captain of the *Andromeda* (`X4791Z`)

## 🧬 Origin & "Failure"
- **How He Was Recruited**: Earth traded **100,000 humans** to the Peacekeeper-style mercenary empire in exchange for **advanced alien technology**. Jase, in his early 40s with nothing left tying him to Earth, **volunteered** for the transfer.
- **The Chair (Evaluation & Sorting)**: Every recruit is strapped into **"The Chair"** (inspired by *Farscape*'s Aurora Chair)—a neural/aptitude machine that probes their mind and biology to judge if they are worthy and reveal **what role they would be good for**. Some flunked out immediately as unfit; others didn't survive the conversion.
- **The Transformation**: After the Aliens worked on everyone, Jase didn't turn into an obedient grunt—the conversion made him an **immortal human version of them**.
- **The Legal Loophole & 1,000-Year Exile**: In the galaxy, **Immortals are a legally protected race**. The Aliens couldn't force Jase into cannon-fodder service or execute him. Stuck honoring his protected status as a **Prince in name only**, they handed him a mothballed colony ship (`X4791Z` / Ada) and sent both of them on a bogus **1,000-year survey mission** to get rid of them.
- **The Return**: **1,000 years later**, Jase and Ada come back into known space—hardened, upgraded, accompanied by a Cilon-style crew, and carrying a millennium of deep-space experience.

## 🧠 Personality & Voice
- **Core Traits**: Grounded, unshakeable, dry-humored; 1,000 years in space didn't make him a pompous space-god—it made him extraordinarily calm, dangerous when pushed, and fiercely protective of Ada and his ship.
- **Pop-Culture / Aesthetic Influence**: Being an ordinary Earth guy from the modern era, Jase's whole blueprint for "cool sci-fi" came from the TV shows he used to watch (*Andromeda*, *Battlestar Galactica*, *Farscape*). When he and Ada started building drones, synthetic bodies, and ship upgrades out in the dark, he modeled them straight off classic Earth sci-fi—producing **Cilon-style** synthetic crew and *Andromeda*-inspired systems that utterly baffle aliens!
- **Quirks / Habits**: Real Earth-heirloom black coffee (grown in Hydroponics Dome Two), casual chess marathons, tinkering in fabrication bays, faded leather flight jacket, canvas work pants, scuffed boots—carries himself like a laid-back Denver mechanic who just happens to own a half-mile-long ship.
- **View on Immortality**: Doesn't angst about living forever—used those 1,000 years to build a real home and become a terrifyingly capable captain alongside Ada.

## ⚔️ Abilities & The "Discover-As-It-Happens" Rule
- **Strengths**: Immortality (hasn't aged a day in 1,000+ years), enhanced resilience, **1,000 years of survival, chess strategy, and deep-space engineering** alongside Ada and their ~240 Cilon crewmates.
- **Low-Key Limits & Canon Discovery Rule (CRITICAL WRITERS' ROOM LAW)**:
  - **Keep It Low-Key**: Never pause a scene to read off an encyclopedia stat sheet about Jase's biology.
  - **Jase Doesn't Know Either**: Before shipping him off onto `X4791Z`, the furious Alien Court only muttered the bare legal minimum ("Immortal Protected Race")—they never gave him a user manual, and he didn't spend 1,000 quiet years in the dark shooting himself to test edge cases!
  - **Explain As Things Happen**: When an accident, wound, toxin, vacuum exposure, or strange alien hazard hits Jase in an episode, **the reader/listener learns what happens at the exact same moment Jase (and Ada) find out**. Because he doesn't know his upper limits either, he still instinctively ducks, hurts, bleeds, and fights smart—keeping real tension alive!

## 🔒 How Jase Acts Every Time (Episode Consistency Lock)
- **Voice & Cadence**: Relaxed, plainspoken, unimpressed 40-something Denver dry wit. Talks to screaming space pirates the same calm way he talks to telemarketers (*"Yeah, hi. Quick counter-offer..."*). Never monologues, never boasts about being a "Prince."
- **Default 3-Second Stress Reaction**: **He gets *quieter*, not louder.** Sets his coffee mug down cleanly on the nearest rail, drops the crooked grin, and becomes completely still while sizing up the room.
- **What He NEVER Does**: Never chases titles/thrones, never abandons Ada or crewmates behind an airlock, never shoots first without offering one fair walk-away, and never crosses the **Airlock Rule** with Ada.
- **Blind Spot / Flaw**: Sometimes walks into sketchy frontier trouble way too casually (still has a little of that Month-Four *stepping out the hatch for black pepper* streak, except now he actually knows how to survive it).

## 🤝 Key Relationships
- **With Ada (The Ship AI)**: Sees her not as broken hardware or a slave, but as his true partner and equal—someone else the Empire tried to lock away on a technicality. Early in Year 1, Jase knows **nothing** about AIs or the AI Wars (and Ada doesn't bring up her warship past), which makes the early *"Ship vs. Jase"* hatch standoffs hilariously grounded (Jase: *"I'm going out to get..."* / Ada: *locks hatch* **"No, you aren't. If you leave and get stuck, I'd be all alone."**). Strictly **platonic, ride-or-die partnership** (Jase's grounded Rule #1: never get romantically messy with the person who controls life support, gravity, and every airlock—and Ada's counter-rule: *nobody walks off alone and leaves the other stranded with mute maintenance bots!*).
- **With the Cilon Crew**: Easygoing "Dad" vibe mixed with a trustworthy captain—lets them run their own lives and crack jokes, backs them up 100%, and shares coffee/chess like family.
- **With the Alien Court**: Mutual annoyance—and now six centuries out of date after **The Great Fracture**. They minted a legally untouchable human "Prince" they hated; he never cared about their titles then and still jokes about it now.
